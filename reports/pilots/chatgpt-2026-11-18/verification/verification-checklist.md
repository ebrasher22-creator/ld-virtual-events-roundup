## Complete the review in three steps

1. Open each event’s organizer page and registration link below. Check all eight items yourself, recording the primary URL and what you observed. A visible button or HTTP response alone does not prove registration works. Inspect the intended session, form, any access restrictions and price. No form was submitted by the assistant. If checking full enrollment needs personal data or creates an attendee registration, decide whether to do that yourself; otherwise keep functionality unresolved.
2. Choose **APPROVE FACTS**, **REJECT**, or **HOLD** for each event, then independently choose **SELECT**, **EXCLUDE**, or **PENDING** for editorial use. Unknown or contradictory facts mean HOLD, not approval. A rejection needs a reason (incorrect date, canceled, paid mismatch, inaccessible registration, weak relevance, etc.). Future events may be fact-approved but must stay out of RW49. An unchecked item never means rejection automatically.
3. Fill the matching entry in `human-review-ledger.DRAFT.json`; preserve a copy in the review packet. Do not copy it to `data/reviews.json` until the authorized canonical workflow has persisted/resolved the exact event. See the ledger handoff instructions below. Returning a completed checklist authorizes only the decisions you explicitly choose; it does not merge or publish.

## Ledger handoff and protection against accidental approval

The existing ledger contract is keyed by event ID, with `record_sha256`, `decision`, `editorial_decision`, `reviewer`, `verified_at`, `score`, and seven `claims` objects, each containing `primary_url` and `observation`. The eight checklist checks map to seven ledger claims because date/year/timezone share `datetime`.

- APPROVE FACTS → `decision: "verified"` **only after your checks**, your name in `reviewer`, and actual check time in RFC3339 `verified_at`. Describe your own observations in every claim, not the assistant’s prose. Record both local and Central times/year in `datetime`.
- REJECT → `decision: "rejected"`, `editorial_decision: "excluded"`; record the reason in the checklist and relevant claim observation. Do not invent a verification timestamp for a rejection.
- HOLD → `decision: "pending"`, `editorial_decision: "pending"`; leave unresolved observations empty and `verified_at` null.
- Editorial SELECT → `editorial_decision: "selected"` only as a distinct human editorial choice. Fact approval alone should keep editorial status pending. Set a numeric score if desired; default 0 does not rank a record as selected. EXCLUDE → `editorial_decision: "excluded"`.

The prepared ledger has **null hashes, null verification timestamps, blank reviewers and observations, and pending decisions**. It is intentionally unable to qualify any event. No placeholder is an approval. `review-manifest.json` provides preview hashes for comparison, not for ledger promotion. Actual canonical records do not exist in `data/` yet. After separately authorized canonical ingestion, registration resolution and assignment, recompute `fingerprint(event)` against the final actual record and fill `record_sha256` on the human-reviewed entry. Any later record change invalidates the hash.

The proposed Intro registration correction is in `proposed-changes.json` and `proposed-discoveries-NOT-INTAKE.json`; the original intake, preview snapshot and real data/review ledger were preserved. Because V1 derives initial IDs from registration URLs and uses URL/title/date reconciliation, do not blindly append the proposed array to intake: that can flag a URL conflict or change a freshly generated identity. Preserve/resolve the stable event identity under human review, then bind the approval to its final ID/hash. Rebind the future-event review if its final ID changes.

Once you return the completed decisions, the next step is an authorized canonical/review update on this review branch. Final publication approval remains separate; no checkbox here authorizes a merge or LinkedIn post.
